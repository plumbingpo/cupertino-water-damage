Emergency Water Damage Restoration Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Place leak sensors near the water heater, washing machine, dishwasher and under sinks.
  • Keep the number of an emergency restoration company saved in your phone before you need it.
  • Run a check on the sump pump ahead of wet weather, and think about adding battery backup.
  • Shut off the water at the main whenever you leave home for more than a few days.
  • Being able to find the electrical panel quickly means wet areas can be de-energized without risk.
  • Insulate pipes in unheated spaces so they do not freeze and split in cold weather.

How to Tell You Need Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Standing water is spreading across floors toward other rooms or down the stairs.
  • Water from a floor above is streaming down interior walls.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, light fixture or electrical outlet.
  • Gallons keep spilling from a ruptured water heater tank.
  • Several hours or more have passed with hardwood, drywall or carpet sitting wet.
  • The breaker box, a running appliance or cords along the floor sit in the path of the water.
  • The water is from a sewer backup or looks dark and smells bad.

How We Handle Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle emergency water damage restoration from start to finish:

1

Answer the Call

A person answers at any hour, asks about the source, the rooms affected and any safety concerns, and tells you what to do while a crew is on the way to your home.

2

Stop the Source

On arrival we check for electrical and contamination hazards, then shut off or isolate whatever is feeding the water. Until the flow ends, no other step does much good.

3

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out by pump and truck-mounted extraction, furniture is shifted to dry ground, and thermal imaging plus meters reveal the moisture inside walls and under floors.

4

Stabilize and Plan

The crew does not go until the drying equipment, meaning air movers and dehumidifiers, is running. We then walk you through what we found, what the next 3 to 5 days of drying look like, and what your insurer will need.
